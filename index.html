Plasma Physics

From single-particle orbits to collective waves, transport, and plasma sheaths.

Contents

Each section states its question, assumptions, and units, and ends with a short knowledge check. Optional derivations are collapsed.

  1. 1

    Introduction, plasma state, and model hierarchy

    Plasma examples, temperature, characteristic lengths and times, and model selection.

  2. 2

    Temperature, entropy, and thermal ionization

    Counted entropy, temperature, Boltzmann weights, and thermal ionization.

  3. 3

    Plasma oscillations

    Electron displacement, the restoring electric field, and the cold plasma frequency.

  4. 4

    Debye shielding

    Boltzmann response, the Debye length, screened potentials, and the plasma parameter.

  5. 5

    Single-particle motion

    Gyromotion, homogeneous-force drifts, guiding centers, mirrors, polarization drift, and cyclotron resonance.

  6. 6

    Kinetic theory of plasmas

    Collisions, distribution functions, phase space, kinetic balance, and model limits.

  7. 7

    Moments of the Boltzmann equation

    Velocity moments, continuity, momentum and energy transport, and closure.

  8. 8

    Multiple-fluid theory of plasmas

    Two-fluid hydrogen plasma, species forces, and diamagnetic current.

  9. 9

    Single-fluid theory and magnetohydrodynamics

    MHD variables, Ohm’s law, frozen flux, diffusion, and equilibrium.

  10. 10

    Collisions and plasma conductivity

    Neutral and Coulomb collisions, Spitzer resistivity, and DC or AC conductivity tensors.

  11. 11

    Plasma diffusion

    Random walks, ambipolar transport, cross-field diffusion, and classical or Bohm-like limits.

  12. 12

    Introduction to waves in plasmas

    Linearization, plasma oscillations, electromagnetic dispersion, and kinetic limits.

  13. 13

    Waves in cold magnetized plasmas

    Cold dielectric tensor, circular and principal modes, oblique propagation, cutoffs, and resonances.

  14. 14

    Collisions, ions, and finite-temperature effects on magnetized waves

    Collisional damping, ion-cyclotron and Alfvén branches, warm dispersion, and model ordering.

  15. 15

    Waves in hot plasmas

    Vlasov response, velocity-space resonances, Landau damping, two-stream growth, and hot magnetized harmonics.

  16. 16

    Plasma sheaths and Langmuir probes

    Particle flux, Bohm sheath entry, floating surfaces, and Langmuir-probe diagnostics.

Glossary

Notation and conventions

SI units
This script uses SI units throughout, with vacuum permittivity 𝜀0 and permeability 𝜇0. For a charge 𝑞, the Lorentz force is 𝑭=𝑞(𝑬+𝒗×𝑩), and electrostatic Poisson’s equation for charge density 𝜌𝑞 is ∇2(Φ)=−𝜌𝑞𝜀0. The symbol 𝜌 without the subscript denotes mass density. Gaussian CGS equivalents are listed only in the CGS translation appendix.
Normalized quantity
A dimensionless quantity formed by dividing by a stated reference scale, such as 𝑥𝜆𝐷 [1] or 𝑡𝜔𝑝 [1]. Dimensional reconstruction is given where the quantity is used.
Statistical notation
Multiplicity is 𝑊 (a dimensionless microstate count), never Ω. Entropy is 𝑆=𝑘𝐵ln𝑊 in JK−1. The ionization energy 𝜒 is in J or eV; scattering angles use 𝜃sc. Chemical potential 𝜇 is an energy per particle; mechanical mobility is 𝜇mob, and magnetic moment is 𝜇mag in JT−1, written 𝜇 in the single-particle chapter. Total kinetic-energy density is 𝐸kin,𝑠 in Jm−3, keeping 𝑊𝑠 available for species multiplicity. Slots 𝑀𝑠 and internal degeneracies 𝑔𝑠 are dimensionless counts. Neutral, ion, and electron densities are 𝑛𝑛, 𝑛𝑖, and 𝑛𝑒 in m−3. The thermal de Broglie wavelength is 𝜆th,𝑠=ℎ2𝜋𝑚𝑠𝑘𝐵𝑇 in m, with Planck’s constant ℎ in Js. For electrons, 𝜆th,𝑒=ℎ𝜋𝑚𝑒𝑣th,𝑒. Thermal momentum 𝑝th,𝑠=2𝑚𝑠𝑘𝐵𝑇 is in kgms−1. A sharp momentum cutoff 𝑝𝑠 is in kgms−1; its estimate 𝜆𝑠=ℎ𝑝𝑠 is in m and is distinct from 𝜆th,𝑠, which uses the Maxwellian-weighted momentum states. Normalized wave frequency uses 𝜔̂, reserving 𝑊 for multiplicity.
Gyrofrequency
The positive rate 𝜔𝑐=|𝑞|𝐵𝑚. The signed quantity Ω=𝑞𝐵𝑚 retains charge orientation. The single-particle chapter follows the lecture notation instead: 𝜔𝑐=𝑞𝛼𝐵/𝑚𝛼 is signed for species 𝛼, |𝜔𝑐| is the positive rate, and the Larmor radius is 𝜌𝐿=𝑣⟂/|𝜔𝑐|.
Debye length
The electron screening length when ions do not respond and the electrons have thermodynamic temperature 𝑇𝑒 is 𝜆𝐷=𝜀0𝑘𝐵𝑇𝑒𝑛𝑒𝑒2. If several classical species respond with Boltzmann densities, 𝜆𝐷−2=∑𝑠𝑛𝑠𝑞𝑠2𝜀0𝑘𝐵𝑇𝑠.
Thermal speed and distribution
The convention is 𝑣th,s=2𝑘𝐵𝑇𝑠𝑚𝑠; a centered Maxwellian varies as exp(−𝑣2𝑣th,s2). The three-velocity distribution satisfies ∫𝑓𝑠d3𝒗=𝑛𝑠. A one-velocity marginal integrates over the other two velocity components; it is not a slice.
Wave convention
Perturbations use exp(𝑖(𝒌⋅𝒓−𝜔𝑡)). With 𝜔=𝜔𝑟+𝑖𝛾, positive 𝛾 means temporal growth. For real frequency and propagation toward increasing 𝑧, 𝑘=𝑘𝑟+𝑖𝑘𝑖 with 𝑘𝑖>0 means spatial attenuation.
Wave-number normalization
The refractive index is 𝑁=𝑘𝑐𝜔 [1]. A different coordinate is 𝐾=𝑘𝑐𝜔𝑝 [1], with 𝜔̂=𝜔𝜔𝑝 [1] and 𝐾=𝑁𝜔̂. In vacuum 𝑁=1, equivalently 𝜔̂=𝐾; these axes are not interchangeable.
Gyroangle
With 𝑩0=𝐵0𝒆𝑧, the usual counterclockwise angle defined by 𝑣𝑥=𝑣⟂cos(𝜃) and 𝑣𝑦=𝑣⟂sin(𝜃) obeys d𝜃d𝑡=−Ω𝑠. The hot-plasma harmonic expansion explicitly uses the opposite orientation; its signed frequency and harmonic labels must be read with that definition.
Closure
An additional constitutive assumption that terminates a moment or reduced-model hierarchy.
