3. Plasma oscillations

A plasma is neutral on average, but its electrons are light and mobile. If a slab of electrons is shifted rigidly against the ions, the separated charge pulls it back, and electron inertia carries it past equilibrium into an oscillation. This cold slab model is the first rigid charge-separation model of the course and supplies the plasma-frequency scale used throughout, following [11,12].

3.1 Electron plasma oscillations

What happens when all electrons in a region are displaced together, faster than thermal motion can rearrange them? Gauss’s law gives the restoring electric field, and that field produces an electron plasma oscillation.

Knowledge check

  1. What supplies the restoring force in the cold plasma oscillator?
    Answer The charge separation between displaced electrons and stationary ions creates an electric field, and the electron charge makes the force oppose the displacement.
  2. Why does the cold model produce a single local frequency rather than a dispersive frequency?
    Answer Pressure and spatial gradients were omitted, so the restoring coefficient contains density and particle constants but no wavenumber.
  3. How would electron collisions change the oscillator qualitatively?
    Answer They introduce momentum loss and therefore damp the oscillation. The undamped harmonic equation is no longer complete.
  4. What is the dimensional status of 𝑡 𝜔 𝑝 , 𝑒 in the animation?
    Answer It is dimensionless because time in seconds is multiplied by an angular frequency in s − 1 .
