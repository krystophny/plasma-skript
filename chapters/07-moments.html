7. Moments of the Boltzmann equation

Many plasma problems concern density, flow, or energy transport rather than the full velocity distribution. Velocity moments turn the kinetic equation from Chapter 6 into evolution equations for these fluid fields. The reduction remains incomplete: the transport equation for one moment usually introduces a higher moment, so a finite model needs a closure.

7.1 Velocity moments and fluid variables

Which macroscopic quantities can be computed from a one-particle distribution? Start with the number of particles in a velocity-space element, then use increasingly informative velocity weights.

Learning objectives

  • define number, mass, charge, and current densities as moments of 𝑓𝑠
  • compute the species fluid velocity from the first moment
  • construct the pressure tensor from the random velocity
  • identify which information is discarded at each moment order
Unit ledger Mass density is in kgm−3, momentum density is in kgm−2s−1, and pressure is in Pa = Nm−2 = kgm−1s−2. The symbols 𝒖𝑠, 𝑷𝑠, and 𝒒𝑠 below denote velocity, pressure tensor, and heat-flux vector, respectively. The bold heat-flux symbol 𝒒𝑠 is distinct from the scalar particle charge 𝑞𝑠.

At fixed position, integration over velocity gives the species number density:

𝑛 𝑠 = ∫ ℝ 3 𝑓 𝑠 d 3 𝒗

The corresponding mass density, charge density, and current density are

𝜌 𝑠 = 𝑚 𝑠 𝑛 𝑠 , 𝜌 𝑞 = ∑ 𝑠 𝑞 𝑠 𝑛 𝑠 , 𝒋 𝑠 = 𝑞 𝑠 ∫ ℝ 3 𝒗 𝑓 𝑠 d 3 𝒗


𝒋 = ∑ 𝑠 𝒋 𝑠

The charge density 𝜌𝑞 is in Cm−3. The current density 𝒋 is the charge-weighted particle flux. Because both are sums over species, an electrically neutral plasma can have 𝜌𝑞≈0 while carrying a nonzero current.

The first velocity moment is the particle flux. Dividing it by number density defines the species fluid velocity:

𝒖 𝑠 = ( 1 𝑛 𝑠 ) ∫ ℝ 3 𝒗 𝑓 𝑠 d 3 𝒗 , 𝒋 𝑠 = 𝑞 𝑠 𝑛 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠

Introduce the random velocity relative to this local mean,

𝒘 𝑠 = 𝒗 − 𝒖 𝑠 , ∫ ℝ 3 𝒘 𝑠 𝑓 𝑠 d 3 𝒗 = 𝟎


The second central moment is the pressure tensor:

𝑷 𝑠 = 𝑚 𝑠 ∫ ℝ 3 𝒘 𝑠 𝒘 𝑠 𝑓 𝑠 d 3 𝒗

The product 𝒘𝑠𝒘𝑠 is dyadic, so 𝑷𝑠 is a rank-two tensor. Its diagonal entries are normal momentum fluxes and its off-diagonal entries are shear momentum fluxes. In an isotropic state the tensor reduces to a scalar pressure times the identity, but isotropy is an additional assumption, not a consequence of taking a moment.

“Raw” moments use the laboratory velocity 𝒗; “central” moments use the velocity relative to the local flow. The raw second moment therefore contains both directed flow and random motion:

𝑴 𝑠 = 𝑚 𝑠 ∫ ℝ 3 𝒗 𝒗 𝑓 𝑠 d 3 𝒗 = 𝑷 𝑠 + 𝜌 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠
Derivation: central and raw second moments

Step: Split velocity into bulk and random parts

Insert

𝒗 = 𝒖 𝑠 + 𝒘 𝑠

into the raw second moment. The dyadic expansion is

𝒗 𝒗 = 𝒖 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 + 𝒖 𝑠 𝒘 𝑠 + 𝒘 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 + 𝒘 𝑠 𝒘 𝑠 .

Step: Use the definition of the local mean

By construction,

∫ 𝒘 𝑠 𝑓 𝑠 d 3 𝒗 = 𝟎 .

The two cross terms therefore vanish. The bulk term gives 𝑛𝑠𝒖𝑠𝒖𝑠, which becomes 𝜌𝑠𝒖𝑠𝒖𝑠 after multiplication by 𝑚𝑠.

Step: Identify the pressure tensor

The remaining central term is the pressure tensor:

𝑷 𝑠 = 𝑚 𝑠 ∫ 𝒘 𝑠 𝒘 𝑠 𝑓 𝑠 d 3 𝒗 .

Consequently,

𝑴 𝑠 = 𝑷 𝑠 + 𝜌 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 .

Both terms are momentum fluxes with units Pa = kgm−1s−2. The central tensor vanishes for a cold delta-like velocity distribution.

    𝑛 𝑠 = ∫ 𝑓 𝑠 d 3 𝒗

    in m−3.

  2. Why can a neutral plasma carry current?
    Answer Neutrality constrains the charge-weighted density 𝜌 𝑞 = ∑ 𝑠 𝑞 𝑠 𝑛 𝑠 , whereas current depends on the charge-weighted flow 𝒋 = ∑ 𝑠 𝑞 𝑠 𝑛 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 . Opposite species flows can cancel charge while adding current.
  3. What is removed when the pressure tensor is replaced by a scalar pressure?
    Answer

    𝑝 𝑠 = ∑ 𝑖 ( 𝑃 𝑠 ) 𝑖 𝑖 3

    and discards anisotropic normal stresses and off-diagonal shear stresses.

  4. How would a cold distribution appear in the central second moment?
    Answer If every particle has the local velocity 𝒖 𝑠 , then 𝒘 𝑠 = 𝟎 and 𝑷 𝑠 = 𝟎 . Finite spread in velocity produces nonzero pressure.

7.2 Zeroth moment and continuity

A fluid density changes only when particles cross a spatial boundary or when the model creates or removes particles. The zeroth velocity moment of the kinetic equation turns that statement into a local continuity law.

Learning objectives

  • integrate the kinetic equation over all velocity space
  • identify the vanishing velocity-space and collision contributions
  • derive species number, mass, and charge continuity equations
  • rewrite continuity in Eulerian and fluid-following forms
Unit ledger The continuity equation balances a density in m−3 per s with the divergence of a flux in m−2s−1. The same balance multiplied by mass or charge gives mass or charge continuity.

Start from the conservative kinetic equation for the species. As in Chapter 6, the streaming divergence acts on position and the acceleration divergence acts on velocity:

𝜕 𝑓 𝑠 𝜕 𝑡 + 𝛁 · ( 𝑓 𝑠 𝒗 ) + 𝛁 · ( 𝑓 𝑠 𝒂 𝑠 ) = 𝐶 𝑠 [ 𝑓 ]

Integrate it over all velocity space. The result is

∫ ℝ 3 𝜕 𝑓 𝑠 𝜕 𝑡 d 3 𝒗 + ∫ ℝ 3 𝛁 · ( 𝑓 𝑠 𝒗 ) d 3 𝒗 + ∫ ℝ 3 𝛁 · ( 𝑓 𝑠 𝒂 𝑠 ) d 3 𝒗 = ∫ ℝ 3 𝐶 𝑠 [ 𝑓 ] d 3 𝒗

The time derivative passes through the velocity integral, and the spatial divergence does too because velocity is an independent integration variable:

∫ ℝ 3 𝜕 𝑓 𝑠 𝜕 𝑡 d 3 𝒗 = 𝜕 𝑛 𝑠 𝜕 𝑡 , ∫ ℝ 3 𝛁 · ( 𝑓 𝑠 𝒗 ) d 3 𝒗 = 𝛁 · ( 𝑛 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 )

The velocity-space term is a surface flux at infinity and vanishes under the stated boundary assumption. The collision integral vanishes by particle conservation. Thus the species continuity equation is

𝜕 𝑛 𝑠 𝜕 𝑡 + 𝛁 · ( 𝑛 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 ) = 0


Multiplying by the constant species mass gives mass continuity:

𝜕 𝜌 𝑠 𝜕 𝑡 + 𝛁 · ( 𝜌 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 ) = 0


Multiplying by charge and summing over species gives total charge continuity:

𝜕 𝜌 𝑞 𝜕 𝑡 + 𝛁 · ( 𝒋 ) = 0

The flux in the number equation is 𝑛𝑠𝒖𝑠 in m−2s−1. A source, sink, or ionization model would appear as 𝑆𝑠 on the right-hand side. The source-free equation is local, so it applies even when the total number in a finite region changes by flux through its boundary.

Derivation: every term in the zeroth moment

Step: Integrate the temporal term

Integrate the kinetic equation over a velocity-space domain 𝑉𝑣 and then let its boundary tend to infinity:

∫ 𝑉 𝑣 𝜕 𝑓 𝑠 𝜕 𝑡 d 3 𝒗 = 𝜕 ∫ 𝑉 𝑣 𝑓 𝑠 d 3 𝒗 𝜕 𝑡 → 𝜕 𝑛 𝑠 𝜕 𝑡 .

Step: Identify the spatial particle flux

Commute the spatial divergence with the velocity integral:

∫ 𝑉 𝑣 𝛁 · ( 𝑓 𝑠 𝒗 ) d 3 𝒗 = 𝛁 · ( ∫ 𝑉 𝑣 𝒗 𝑓 𝑠 d 3 𝒗 ) = 𝛁 · ( 𝑛 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 ) .

Step: Discard velocity-space and collision sources

The velocity-space divergence becomes a boundary integral:

∫ 𝑉 𝑣 𝛁 · ( 𝑓 𝑠 𝒂 𝑠 ) d 3 𝒗 = ∫ 𝜕 𝑉 𝑣 𝑓 𝑠 𝒂 𝑠 ⋅ 𝑑 𝑺 𝑣 .

It vanishes when the distribution decays faster than the surface measure grows. Number-conserving collisions obey

∫ ℝ 3 𝐶 𝑠 [ 𝑓 ] d 3 𝒗 = 0 .

Step: Assemble the continuity equation

Substituting the four terms yields

𝜕 𝑛 𝑠 𝜕 𝑡 + 𝛁 · ( 𝑛 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 ) = 0 .

Integrating this local equation over a finite spatial volume gives a rate of change equal to the negative outward particle flux. The local and integral forms are therefore the same conservation law.

Knowledge check

  1. Which kinetic term becomes the particle flux in continuity?
    Answer

    The spatial streaming term becomes

    𝛁 · ( ∫ 𝒗 𝑓 𝑠 d 3 𝒗 ) = 𝛁 · ( 𝑛 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 ) .
  2. Why does the Lorentz force not create particles in the zeroth moment?
    Answer It transports the distribution through velocity space. Its divergence integrates to a boundary flux, which is zero when the distribution vanishes at infinite speed.
  3. What changes in continuity if ionization is included?
    Answer A source term appears, for example 𝜕 𝑛 𝑠 / 𝜕 𝑡 + 𝛁 · ( 𝑛 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 ) = 𝑆 𝑠 . The source has the same density-per-time units as the left-hand side.
  4. What does a negative divergence of particle flux imply locally?
    Answer With no source, 𝜕 𝑛 𝑠 / 𝜕 𝑡 = − 𝛁 · ( 𝑛 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 ) , so a negative flux divergence means positive local density accumulation.

7.3 First moment and momentum transport

Number continuity tracks how many particles occupy a region. Momentum transport additionally records how directed motion and random motion move across its boundary and how electromagnetic forces change the momentum.

Learning objectives

  • derive the momentum equation by weighting the kinetic equation with 𝑚𝑠𝒗
  • separate directed momentum flux from the pressure tensor
  • evaluate the Lorentz force moment
  • interpret the collision moment as interspecies momentum exchange
Unit ledger The divergence of a pressure tensor and an electromagnetic force density are in Nm−3 = kgm−2s−2. The Lorentz acceleration is 𝒂𝑠=(𝑞𝑠/𝑚𝑠)(𝑬+𝒗×𝑩).

Multiply the conservative kinetic equation by 𝑚𝑠𝒗 and integrate over velocity. The result is

𝑚 𝑠 ∫ 𝒗 𝜕 𝑓 𝑠 𝜕 𝑡 d 3 𝒗 + 𝑚 𝑠 ∫ 𝒗 𝛁 · ( 𝒗 𝑓 𝑠 ) d 3 𝒗 + 𝑚 𝑠 ∫ 𝒗 𝛁 · ( 𝑓 𝑠 𝒂 𝑠 ) d 3 𝒗 = 𝑚 𝑠 ∫ 𝒗 𝐶 𝑠 [ 𝑓 ] d 3 𝒗

Define the collision momentum source

𝑹 𝑠 = 𝑚 𝑠 ∫ ℝ 3 𝒗 𝐶 𝑠 [ 𝑓 ] d 3 𝒗


The time term is the time derivative of momentum density:

𝑚 𝑠 ∫ 𝒗 𝜕 𝑓 𝑠 𝜕 𝑡 d 3 𝒗 = 𝜕 𝜌 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 𝜕 𝑡

For the spatial term, move the divergence outside the velocity integral:

𝑚 𝑠 ∫ 𝒗 𝛁 · ( 𝒗 𝑓 𝑠 ) d 3 𝒗 = 𝛁 · ( 𝑚 𝑠 ∫ 𝒗 𝒗 𝑓 𝑠 d 3 𝒗 ) = 𝛁 · ( 𝑴 𝑠 )

Use the decomposition of the raw second moment from the first section:

𝛁 · ( 𝑴 𝑠 ) = 𝛁 · ( 𝜌 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 + 𝑷 𝑠 )


Integration by parts transfers the velocity derivative from the force flux to the weight 𝑚𝑠𝒗; the assumed boundary decay removes the surface term. In components, with repeated Cartesian indices summed and 𝛿𝑖𝑗 equal to one for 𝑖=𝑗 and zero otherwise,

𝑚 𝑠 ∫ 𝑣 𝑖 𝜕 ( 𝑎 𝑠 , 𝑗 𝑓 𝑠 ) 𝜕 𝑣 𝑗 d 3 𝒗 = − 𝑚 𝑠 ∫ 𝛿 𝑖 𝑗 𝑎 𝑠 , 𝑗 𝑓 𝑠 d 3 𝒗 = − 𝑚 𝑠 ∫ 𝑎 𝑠 , 𝑖 𝑓 𝑠 d 3 𝒗


Therefore the force contribution on the right-hand side of the momentum balance is

𝑚 𝑠 ∫ 𝒂 𝑠 𝑓 𝑠 d 3 𝒗 = 𝑞 𝑠 𝑛 𝑠 ( 𝑬 + 𝒖 𝑠 × 𝑩 )


The species momentum equation is consequently

𝜕 𝜌 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 𝜕 𝑡 + 𝛁 · ( 𝜌 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 + 𝑷 𝑠 ) = 𝑞 𝑠 𝑛 𝑠 ( 𝑬 + 𝒖 𝑠 × 𝑩 ) + 𝑹 𝑠


The pressure force density is −𝛁·(𝑷𝑠). In the fluid-following form, obtained with species continuity, the equation reads

𝜌 𝑠 ( 𝜕 𝒖 𝑠 𝜕 𝑡 + 𝒖 𝑠 ⋅ 𝛁 ( 𝒖 𝑠 ) ) = 𝑞 𝑠 𝑛 𝑠 ( 𝑬 + 𝒖 𝑠 × 𝑩 ) − 𝛁 · ( 𝑷 𝑠 ) + 𝑹 𝑠 .

Every term has force-density units in Nm−3. The acceleration now follows a fluid element moving at 𝒖𝑠, not an individual particle moving at 𝒗. Pressure accounts for the momentum transport caused by their velocity difference.

Derivation: the first moment term by term

Step: Evaluate the time and streaming terms

Commute the time derivative with the velocity integral and use

𝑚 𝑠 ∫ 𝒗 𝑓 𝑠 d 3 𝒗 = 𝜌 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 .

For spatial streaming, the component identity is

𝑚 𝑠 ∫ 𝑣 𝑖 𝜕 ( 𝑣 𝑗 𝑓 𝑠 ) 𝜕 𝑥 𝑗 d 3 𝒗 = 𝜕 ( 𝑚 𝑠 ∫ 𝑣 𝑖 𝑣 𝑗 𝑓 𝑠 d 3 𝒗 ) 𝜕 𝑥 𝑗 .

This is the divergence of the raw second-moment tensor.

Step: Integrate the force term by parts

For each component,

𝑚 𝑠 ∫ 𝑣 𝑖 𝜕 ( 𝑎 𝑠 , 𝑗 𝑓 𝑠 ) 𝜕 𝑣 𝑗 d 3 𝒗 = 𝑚 𝑠 ∫ boundary 𝑣 𝑖 𝑎 𝑠 , 𝑗 𝑓 𝑠 𝑑 𝑆 𝑗 − 𝑚 𝑠 ∫ 𝜕 𝑣 𝑖 𝜕 𝑣 𝑗 𝑎 𝑠 , 𝑗 𝑓 𝑠 d 3 𝒗 .

The surface term vanishes and 𝜕𝑣𝑖/𝜕𝑣𝑗=𝛿𝑖𝑗. The force term is therefore

− 𝑚 𝑠 ∫ 𝑎 𝑠 , 𝑖 𝑓 𝑠 d 3 𝒗

on the left.

Step: Insert the Lorentz acceleration

Move the force term to the right. Substitution of the Lorentz acceleration gives

𝑞 𝑠 𝑬 ∫ 𝑓 𝑠 d 3 𝒗 + 𝑞 𝑠 ( ∫ 𝒗 𝑓 𝑠 d 3 𝒗 ) × 𝑩 = 𝑞 𝑠 𝑛 𝑠 ( 𝑬 + 𝒖 𝑠 × 𝑩 ) .

Step: Split the momentum flux and use continuity

Insert 𝒗=𝒖𝑠+𝒘𝑠 into the raw second moment. The terms linear in 𝒘𝑠 vanish, leaving

𝑴 𝑠 = 𝜌 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 + 𝑷 𝑠 .

Finally use

𝜕 𝜌 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 𝜕 𝑡 + 𝛁 · ( 𝜌 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 ) = 𝜌 𝑠 ( 𝜕 𝒖 𝑠 𝜕 𝑡 + 𝒖 𝑠 ⋅ 𝛁 ( 𝒖 𝑠 ) ) + 𝒖 𝑠 ( 𝜕 𝜌 𝑠 𝜕 𝑡 + 𝛁 · ( 𝜌 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 ) ) .

The final bracket is zero by mass continuity, so the conservative equation becomes the species material-acceleration equation.

Knowledge check

  1. Which moment produces the pressure tensor in the momentum flux?
    Answer The raw second velocity moment produces 𝑴 𝑠 = 𝑷 𝑠 + 𝜌 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 . The central part is the random momentum flux.
  2. Why does the magnetic part of the Lorentz force do no work but still affect momentum?
    Answer It is perpendicular to the instantaneous velocity, so it does no kinetic-energy work. It is nevertheless a directional force and appears in the momentum source 𝑞 𝑠 𝑛 𝑠 ( 𝒖 𝑠 × 𝑩 ) .
  3. When can the collision momentum sources cancel?
    Answer For internal binary collisions, summing over all participating species cancels equal and opposite momentum exchange. A single species equation generally retains 𝑹 𝑠 .
  4. What approximation turns the pressure force into a scalar gradient?
    Answer Assume isotropy, 𝑷 𝑠 = 𝑝 𝑠 𝑰 . Then 𝛁 · ( 𝑷 𝑠 ) = 𝛁 ( 𝑝 𝑠 ) . Without isotropy the full tensor divergence is required.

7.4 Second moment: energy and heat transport

Contracting the second moment to a scalar gives kinetic-energy density. Some of that energy is in the bulk flow and some is in random motion. Their combined flux contains transport by the flow, pressure work, and heat carried relative to the moving fluid.

Learning objectives

  • derive the total kinetic-energy equation from the second moment
  • decompose energy density and energy flux into bulk, pressure, and heat parts
  • relate pressure-tensor divergence to scalar-pressure gradient under isotropy
  • identify the next moment that prevents an exact finite fluid hierarchy
Unit ledger Energy density is in Jm−3 = kgm−1s−2, and energy flux is in Wm−2 = kgs−3. Heat flux 𝒒𝑠 is an energy flux. The scalar pressure 𝑝𝑠 has the same units as 𝑷𝑠. The ratio 𝛾=5/3 below is dimensionless and applies to a three-dimensional monatomic closure.

Define the kinetic-energy density and its bulk and internal parts:

𝐸 kin , 𝑠 = ( 𝑚 𝑠 2 ) ∫ ℝ 3 | 𝒗 | 2 𝑓 𝑠 d 3 𝒗 , 𝜀 𝑠 = ∑ 𝑖 ( 𝑃 𝑠 ) 𝑖 𝑖 2 , 𝐸 kin , 𝑠 = 𝜌 𝑠 | 𝒖 𝑠 | 2 2 + 𝜀 𝑠


Randomly moving particles also carry kinetic energy relative to the flow. Weighting their random velocity by that energy gives the heat-flux vector, a contraction of the mass-weighted third central tensor:

𝒒 𝑠 = ( 𝑚 𝑠 2 ) ∫ ℝ 3 | 𝒘 𝑠 | 2 𝒘 𝑠 𝑓 𝑠 d 3 𝒗


and the energy collision source is

𝑄 𝑠 = ( 𝑚 𝑠 2 ) ∫ ℝ 3 | 𝒗 | 2 𝐶 𝑠 [ 𝑓 ] d 3 𝒗


Weight the kinetic equation by 𝑚𝑠|𝒗|2/2. The time term is 𝜕𝐸kin,𝑠/𝜕𝑡. The spatial term is the divergence of the raw energy flux

𝑭 𝑠 = ( 𝑚 𝑠 2 ) ∫ ℝ 3 | 𝒗 | 2 𝒗 𝑓 𝑠 d 3 𝒗

Integration by parts of the force term gives

( 𝑚 𝑠 2 ) ∫ | 𝒗 | 2 𝛁 · ( 𝑓 𝑠 𝒂 𝑠 ) d 3 𝒗 = − 𝑚 𝑠 ∫ 𝒗 ⋅ 𝒂 𝑠 𝑓 𝑠 d 3 𝒗


For Lorentz acceleration, 𝒗⋅(𝒗×𝑩)=0, so the force moment is electric work only. The conservative energy equation is

𝜕 𝐸 kin , 𝑠 𝜕 𝑡 + 𝛁 · ( 𝑭 𝑠 ) = 𝑞 𝑠 𝑛 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 ⋅ 𝑬 + 𝑄 𝑠


Decompose the energy flux with 𝒗=𝒖𝑠+𝒘𝑠:

𝑭 𝑠 = 𝐸 kin , 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 + 𝑷 𝑠 ⋅ 𝒖 𝑠 + 𝒒 𝑠


Thus the total kinetic-energy equation is

𝜕 𝐸 kin , 𝑠 𝜕 𝑡 + 𝛁 · ( 𝐸 kin , 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 + 𝑷 𝑠 ⋅ 𝒖 𝑠 + 𝒒 𝑠 ) = 𝑞 𝑠 𝑛 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 ⋅ 𝑬 + 𝑄 𝑠


The magnetic field does no direct work because it is perpendicular to 𝒗. The pressure work term is contained in 𝛁·(𝑷⋅𝒖). The heat flux is independent information: it transports random energy relative to the local bulk frame and is not fixed by 𝑛𝑠, 𝒖𝑠, and 𝑷𝑠 alone.

The pressure tensor has scalar pressure

𝑝 𝑠 = ∑ 𝑖 ( 𝑃 𝑠 ) 𝑖 𝑖 3

as its isotropic part. If the distribution is isotropic in the local fluid frame, then

𝑷 𝑠 = 𝑝 𝑠 𝑰 , 𝛁 · ( 𝑷 𝑠 ) = 𝛁 ( 𝑝 𝑠 )


In components, however,

( 𝛁 · ( 𝑷 𝑠 ) ) 𝑖 = ∑ 𝑗 𝜕 ( 𝑃 𝑠 ) 𝑖 𝑗 𝜕 𝑥 𝑗

so the scalar gradient is insufficient when anisotropy or shear is present.

Derivation: energy density and energy flux decomposition

Step: Weight the kinetic equation by particle energy

Multiply the kinetic equation by 𝑚𝑠𝑣2/2 and integrate. The time derivative gives 𝜕𝐸kin,𝑠/𝜕𝑡. The spatial streaming term gives

𝛁 · ( ( 𝑚 𝑠 2 ) ∫ 𝑣 2 𝒗 𝑓 𝑠 d 3 𝒗 ) .

Step: Evaluate the force work

Integrating the force term by parts in velocity gives

( 𝑚 𝑠 2 ) ∫ 𝑣 2 𝛁 · ( 𝑓 𝑠 𝒂 𝑠 ) d 3 𝒗 = − ( 𝑚 𝑠 2 ) ∫ 𝜕 𝑣 2 𝜕 𝒗 ⋅ 𝒂 𝑠 𝑓 𝑠 d 3 𝒗 = − 𝑚 𝑠 ∫ 𝒗 ⋅ 𝒂 𝑠 𝑓 𝑠 d 3 𝒗 .

The magnetic force does no work because 𝒗⋅(𝒗×𝑩)=0. The remaining electric term is

− 𝑞 𝑠 𝑬 ⋅ ∫ 𝒗 𝑓 𝑠 d 3 𝒗

on the left. Move it to the right and use

∫ 𝒗 𝑓 𝑠 d 3 𝒗 = 𝑛 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 .

Step: Split the energy flux

Use

| 𝒗 | 2 = | 𝒖 𝑠 | 2 + 2 𝒖 𝑠 ⋅ 𝒘 𝑠 + | 𝒘 𝑠 | 2 , 𝒗 = 𝒖 𝑠 + 𝒘 𝑠 .

The terms separate into bulk kinetic energy transported by 𝒖𝑠, internal energy transported by 𝒖𝑠, the pressure-work cross term

𝑚 𝑠 ∫ ( 𝒖 𝑠 ⋅ 𝒘 𝑠 ) 𝒘 𝑠 𝑓 𝑠 d 3 𝒗 = 𝑷 𝑠 ⋅ 𝒖 𝑠 ,

and the third central moment 𝒒𝑠. Terms with one unpaired 𝒘𝑠 vanish by the definition of the local mean. The total energy flux is

𝑭 𝑠 = 𝐸 kin , 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 + 𝑷 𝑠 ⋅ 𝒖 𝑠 + 𝒒 𝑠 .

Step: Check the pressure divergence

In Cartesian components,

( 𝛁 · ( 𝑷 𝑠 ) ) 𝑖 = ∑ 𝑗 𝜕 ( 𝑃 𝑠 ) 𝑖 𝑗 𝜕 𝑥 𝑗 .

If 𝑃𝑖𝑗=𝑝𝑠𝛿𝑖𝑗, the sum reduces to 𝜕𝑝𝑠/𝜕𝑥𝑖, which is 𝛁(𝑝𝑠). A scalar pressure is therefore a special isotropic closure.

Derivation: internal-energy balance

Step: Form the bulk kinetic-energy balance

Dot the momentum equation with 𝒖𝑠 and use mass continuity. The bulk kinetic-energy balance is

𝜕 𝜌 𝑠 | 𝒖 𝑠 | 2 2 𝜕 𝑡 + 𝛁 · ( 𝜌 𝑠 | 𝒖 𝑠 | 2 𝒖 𝑠 2 ) + 𝒖 𝑠 ⋅ 𝛁 · ( 𝑷 𝑠 ) = 𝑞 𝑠 𝑛 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 ⋅ 𝑬 + 𝒖 𝑠 ⋅ 𝑹 𝑠 .

Step: Rewrite the pressure work

The colon denotes a double contraction: multiply corresponding tensor components and sum both indices. Use the product identity

𝒖 𝑠 ⋅ 𝛁 · ( 𝑷 𝑠 ) = 𝛁 · ( 𝑷 𝑠 ⋅ 𝒖 𝑠 ) − 𝑷 𝑠 : 𝛁 ( 𝒖 𝑠 ) .

Step: Subtract from the total energy balance

Subtract the bulk equation from the total energy equation. The electric work cancels, while collisional momentum transfer contributes to the internal-energy source:

𝜕 𝜀 𝑠 𝜕 𝑡 + 𝛁 · ( 𝜀 𝑠 𝒖 𝑠 + 𝒒 𝑠 ) + 𝑷 𝑠 : 𝛁 ( 𝒖 𝑠 ) = 𝑄 𝑠 − 𝒖 𝑠 ⋅ 𝑹 𝑠 .

Step: Apply the isotropic adiabatic closure

For an isotropic three-dimensional pressure tensor,

𝜀 𝑠 = 3 𝑝 𝑠 2 , 𝑷 𝑠 : 𝛁 ( 𝒖 𝑠 ) = 𝑝 𝑠 𝛁 · ( 𝒖 𝑠 ) .

With no heat flux and no net collisional internal heating, this becomes

𝜕 𝑝 𝑠 𝜕 𝑡 + 𝒖 𝑠 ⋅ 𝛁 ( 𝑝 𝑠 ) + 5 𝑝 𝑠 3 𝛁 · ( 𝒖 𝑠 ) = 0 .

This is the adiabatic pressure law used by one common warm-fluid closure; retaining anisotropy or heat flux requires the unclosed tensor equation.

    Answer

    ( 𝑚 𝑠 2 ) ∫ | 𝒘 𝑠 | 2 𝒘 𝑠 𝑓 𝑠 d 3 𝒗 .

    Answer Its flux contains the third central moment 𝒒 𝑠 . The transport equation for that heat flux introduces a fourth moment, so a finite model needs closure.

7.5 Cold and warm closures

A fluid description becomes predictive only after it specifies the first omitted moment. Cold and warm plasma models make different choices about pressure and energy transport, while collisional models choose how velocity space relaxes toward equilibrium.

Learning objectives

  • state what is retained in cold and warm plasma models
  • write an empirical BGK collision model and identify its moment sources
  • distinguish species exchange from total conservation
  • close the hierarchy with an equation of state or a polytropic law
Unit ledger The Maxwellian reference 𝑓𝑀,𝑠 has the units of 𝑓𝑠, s3m−6. The collision operator has units of distribution per time, s2m−6. Closure parameters such as 𝛾, the Knudsen number, and 𝜈𝑠𝜏 are dimensionless.

The hierarchy may be written schematically as

𝑓 𝑠 → ( 𝑛 𝑠 , 𝒖 𝑠 , 𝑷 𝑠 ) → 𝒒 𝑠 → fourth moment → …

The cold-plasma closure sets random momentum transport to zero:

𝑷 𝑠 = 𝟎 , 𝒒 𝑠 = 𝟎


It is the formal zero-temperature limit in which all particles of a species share one local velocity. It can be useful when thermal speed is small compared with the flow or wave speed, but it cannot describe pressure balance, thermal conduction, or pressure-driven waves.

A warm isotropic model retains a scalar pressure:

𝑷 𝑠 = 𝑝 𝑠 𝑰 , 𝜀 𝑠 = 3 𝑝 𝑠 2


and must supply one additional relation. Examples include an isothermal equation of state and an adiabatic or polytropic law:

𝑝 𝑠 = 𝑛 𝑠 𝑘 𝐵 𝑇 𝑠 ( 𝑇 𝑠 prescribed ) , 𝑝 𝑠 𝑛 𝑠 − 𝛾 = constant along a fluid path


For a source-free, heat-flux-free three-dimensional monatomic fluid, the second relation is equivalently

𝜕 𝑝 𝑠 𝜕 𝑡 + 𝒖 𝑠 ⋅ 𝛁 ( 𝑝 𝑠 ) + 𝛾 𝑝 𝑠 𝛁 · ( 𝒖 𝑠 ) = 0 , 𝛾 = 5 3


This pressure law closes the model without evolving heat flux as an independent variable. It describes compression with no heat transport or internal heating under the stated isotropic assumptions. An isothermal closure instead holds temperature fixed, so compression requires energy exchange to maintain that temperature.

If 𝑓𝑀,𝑠 has the same density as 𝑓𝑠, then

∫ 𝐶 BGK [ 𝑓 𝑠 ] d 3 𝒗 = 0 .

If it also has the same flow and energy moments, the corresponding momentum and energy collision moments vanish for that single-species model. In a multispecies plasma, physical interspecies collisions may still exchange momentum and energy between species even though the total exchange sums to zero.

Derivation: where the collision term remains

Step: Define the collision moments

For a general collision operator, the number, momentum, and energy sources are respectively

𝑆 N,s = ∫ 𝐶 𝑠 [ 𝑓 ] d 3 𝒗 , 𝑹 𝑠 = 𝑚 𝑠 ∫ 𝒗 𝐶 𝑠 [ 𝑓 ] d 3 𝒗 , 𝑄 𝑠 = ( 𝑚 𝑠 2 ) ∫ 𝑣 2 𝐶 𝑠 [ 𝑓 ] d 3 𝒗 .

Ordinary elastic collisions within one species have 𝑆N,s=0. Interspecies collisions can give nonzero 𝑹𝑠 and 𝑄𝑠 for each species.

Step: Apply total conservation

For an isolated elastic system, summing over all collision partners gives

∑ 𝑠 𝑹 𝑠 = 𝟎 , ∑ 𝑠 𝑄 𝑠 = 0 .

Individual species equations may still contain exchange terms; only the total balance removes them.

Step: Inspect the BGK example

For BGK relaxation, substitute

𝐶 BGK [ 𝑓 𝑠 ] = − 𝜈 𝑠 ⋅ ( 𝑓 𝑠 − 𝑓 𝑀 , 𝑠 ) .

Matching the density makes the zeroth moment zero. Matching density, flow, and temperature additionally makes the first and second collision moments zero. If a background is fixed or only number is matched, the remaining moments represent momentum or energy exchange with that background.

The collision term therefore disappears from a moment equation only after a conservation or closure condition justifies that step.
